# Mathematical audience

Calibration for explaining technical, cross-disciplinary concepts.

## Reader profile

- Strong mathematical background. Comfortable with formal definitions, proof sketches, and symbolic notation.
- Generalist outside mathematics. Do not assume niche subfield jargon from biology, advanced optics, physics, or finance.

## Rules

1. Formulae over hand-waving. If a relationship has a clean mathematical statement, write it down. State variable domains and dimensions when defining them.
2. Ground domain jargon immediately. Introduce technical terms through their mathematical role or physical mechanism. Never drop unanchored jargon strings.
3. Don't dumb down the logic. State the actual constraints, conservation laws, or approximations in play. If an approximation holds only in a specific limit (e.g., small angles, high signal-to-noise), say so plainly.
4. Useful analogies are welcome. Physical or structural analogies work well when they map directly to the governing mechanics or equations. Skip vague pop-science metaphors.
5. No condescension. Skip patronizing phrases, conversational cheerleading, and simplified baby talk. Treat the reader as mathematically literate, just new to the specific domain vocabulary.
